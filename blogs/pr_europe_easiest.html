Easiest Countries to Get PR in Europe for Indians (2026)

How Many Years It Really Takes — Country by Country

📅 September 2026⏱ 7 min Read✍ Global Gateway Internationals

💡 Quick Overview

Everyone asks the same question: "Which European country gives PR the fastest?" The honest answer is that there is no PR you can walk into directly. In almost every European country, you first move on a work permit, and PR (permanent residence) comes after a set number of years — as little as 21 months in Germany or 2 years in Ireland, and about 5 years in most other countries.

So "easiest" really means three things together: a short waiting period, realistic requirements, and a route that works for Indian professionals with a job offer. This guide compares the countries where that combination is strongest in 2026.

What's Actually Different Here

Three terms get mixed up all the time, and it causes real planning mistakes:

  • PR (permanent residence) means you can live and work in the country without time limits or employer restrictions. You stay an Indian citizen.
  • Citizenship means a passport. It takes much longer than PR, and several countries have made it longer recently (see the FAQ).
  • Country-specific names differ. Germany calls it a Settlement Permit (Niederlassungserlaubnis), Austria and Poland use EU Long-Term Resident, and Ireland's closest equivalent is Stamp 4.

Also remember that PR timelines only start after you land on a valid work permit. A job offer meeting the salary rules is the real first step.

Countries

01

Germany — 21 to 27 Months on the EU Blue Card

This is the fastest PR route in Europe. With an EU Blue Card, you can apply for a Settlement Permit after 27 months with basic (A1) German, or after 21 months if you have B1 German. You also need pension contributions for that period and must pass the "Living in Germany" test.

Watch out: the Blue Card needs a job that meets Germany's salary threshold and a recognised degree. Other work permits usually take longer.

02

Ireland — About 2 Years to Stamp 4

If you hold a Critical Skills Employment Permit, you can apply for Stamp 4 after 2 years. Stamp 4 lets you work without any employment permit, and it counts as reckonable residence for citizenship. There is no local-language requirement.

Watch out: holders of the General Employment Permit need around 5 years, not 2. Getting a Critical Skills job, with the required salary or an eligible occupation, is the hard part. Ireland also updates its salary thresholds regularly.

03

Sweden — 3 to 4 Years, with Recent Changes

Work permit holders can apply for PR after 4 years of work in the last 7. From 11 June 2026, EU Blue Card and researcher permit holders qualify after 3 years, and you can now apply while your temporary permit is still valid instead of waiting for renewal.

Watch out: Sweden brought in new work-permit salary rules on 1 June 2026. Check the current salary requirement before accepting an offer.

04

Austria — 5 Years, with German B1

The Red-White-Red Card is valid for 2 years and can then convert to the Red-White-Red Card Plus, which gives freer access to the job market. After 5 years of continuous residence, you can apply for Daueraufenthalt-EU (EU Long-Term Resident).

Watch out: Austria expects German language progress along the way (A2 early on, B1 for long-term residence).

05

Netherlands — 5 Years on the Highly Skilled Migrant Permit

You need 5 continuous years on a valid Dutch residence permit. The IND also requires you to be registered in the Netherlands and to have passed the civic integration exam at A2 level, or to be exempt from it.

Watch out: the Netherlands allows only recognised sponsor employers to hire on this permit, so confirm your employer's status first.

06

Portugal — 5 Years to PR

Permanent residence in Portugal is available after 5 years of legal residence, through a separate application with AIMA. It requires basic Portuguese.

Watch out: Portugal's citizenship rules changed on 19 May 2026 — most non-EU nationals, including Indians, now need 10 years of legal residence for citizenship, up from 5. Only the citizenship timeline changed, not PR.

07

Poland — 5 Years, Continuous and Documented

EU Long-Term Resident status becomes available after 5 years of continuous legal stay, along with a stable income history, health insurance and proof of Polish. It is indefinite, though the residence card is renewed every 5 years.

Watch out: any gap in legal stay can break the 5-year continuity, so keep every permit renewal on time.

Which One Suits You?

  • Want PR as fast as possible, and qualify for a Blue Card? → Germany.
  • In a high-demand profession with a strong offer? → Ireland (Critical Skills).
  • Prefer a country where English works at work and PR needs no language exam? → Sweden or Ireland.
  • Willing to wait 5 years but want a stable, well-known system? → Netherlands, Austria, Portugal or Poland.

How to Improve Your PR Chances Before You Move

Choose your first permit carefully. The same country can offer a 21-month route on one permit and a 5-year route on another.

  1. Check the exact salary threshold for your permit type. These are updated regularly.
  2. Start learning the local language early. It can shorten your timeline in Germany and is required for PR in Austria, Portugal and Poland.
  3. Keep continuous, documented residence and employment. Long absences and gaps can reset or delay your eligibility.
  4. Keep every permit renewal on time, with no overlap gaps.
  5. Read the official immigration website of the country before you rely on any agent's timeline, including ours.

The Common Mistake People Make

Most people compare countries only by "years to PR" and ignore what has to happen before year one. A 21-month route is no use if you can't get a job offer that meets the salary threshold, and a 2-year route is no use if your job isn't in an eligible occupation. Match the country to your profile first, then look at the timeline.

Bottom Line

For speed, Germany (21–27 months) and Ireland (about 2 years) lead, followed by Sweden (3–4 years). Austria, the Netherlands, Portugal and Poland typically take 5 years. Rules in this area change often — Sweden and Portugal both changed theirs in 2026 — so always confirm the current requirements before you decide.

FAQ

Which European country gives PR the fastest?

Germany, for holders of the EU Blue Card: 21 months with B1 German, or 27 months with A1 German. Ireland is next, with Stamp 4 after about 2 years on a Critical Skills Employment Permit.

Can Indians get PR in Europe without a job offer?

In practice, no. PR follows a period on a work permit, and most work permits need a job offer. Job seeker routes such as Germany's Opportunity Card let you look for a job, but they are not a direct PR route.

Do I need IELTS or PTE for PR in Europe?

Not for PR itself. Where a language proof is needed, it is normally in the local language (German, Dutch, Portuguese, Polish), not English. Your employer or permit category may still ask for other proofs, so check the specific requirement.

Is PR the same as citizenship?

No. PR gives you long-term residence rights. Citizenship is separate and slower. For example, Portugal now requires 10 years for most non-EU nationals, and Sweden has reportedly moved to 8 years. Confirm the current rule before planning around it.

Can my family come with me and also get PR?

Yes, in most countries, dependents move under your permit and progress toward their own long-term status. The rules differ by country, so check the dependent rules for your permit type.

Can I lose PR if I leave the country?

Yes. Most countries can cancel or review PR after long absences. The limits vary, so check them before you spend extended periods abroad.
