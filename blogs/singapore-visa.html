Singapore Employment Pass vs S Pass vs Work Permit: Which One Fits You?

Salary, Quota and Eligibility Rules Compared

📅 October 2026 ⏱ 7 min Read ✍ Global Gateway Internationals

💡 Quick Overview

Singapore does not have just one work visa. It has three main work passes, and each one is for a different type of worker.

  • Employment Pass (EP): for managers, engineers and other professionals.
  • S Pass: for mid-level skilled staff, like technicians.
  • Work Permit: for semi-skilled workers in certain sectors, like construction.

You cannot simply choose the pass you like. Your salary, your education, your job and your employer decide which pass you can get.

Also note that the minimum salary for EP and S Pass will go up for new applications from 1 January 2027. So if you plan to apply soon, the date matters.

Note: S$ means Singapore dollars. Rupee amounts in brackets are approximate, based on S$1 = ₹74 (21 October 2026). The rate changes every day. All salary amounts are per month.

What's Actually Different Here

Many people think the three passes are just low, medium and high. It is not that simple. Each pass has its own test.

EP: Your salary must be above the minimum first. Then you need 40 points in a points test called COMPASS.

S Pass: The minimum salary is lower. But your employer must also have space under their S Pass quota. A quota is a limit on how many S Pass holders they can hire.

Work Permit: This depends on the sector, your job and your country. There is no age-based salary table.

One thing is the same for all three. Your employer applies for the pass, not you. And no pass is given without a confirmed job. Nobody can sell you a pass.

The Three Passes Explained

01

Employment Pass (EP): For Managers, Engineers and Professionals

The EP is for professionals. First, your monthly salary must be above the minimum for your age. The minimum goes up as you get older. Then you need at least 40 points in COMPASS, unless you are exempt. You get points for your salary, your education, how mixed the employer's team is, whether the employer supports hiring local people, and whether your job is in short supply in Singapore.

Right now, the minimum is S$5,600 (about ₹4.14 lakh) for age 23 and below, and S$10,700 (about ₹7.92 lakh) for age 45 and above. For financial services jobs, it is S$6,200 (about ₹4.59 lakh) to S$11,800 (about ₹8.73 lakh).

From 1 January 2027, it becomes S$6,000 (about ₹4.44 lakh) to S$11,500 (about ₹8.51 lakh). For financial services, it becomes S$6,600 (about ₹4.88 lakh) to S$12,700 (about ₹9.40 lakh).

Watch out: the salary minimum is a hard rule. If your salary is below it, extra points will not help. A degree gives you points, but you can also reach 40 points in other ways. The employer must also advertise the job in Singapore first, as required by the Fair Consideration Framework.

02

S Pass: For Mid-Level Skilled Staff

The S Pass is for mid-level skilled staff. The minimum salary also goes up with age. Right now, it is S$3,300 (about ₹2.44 lakh) for age 23 and below, and S$4,800 (about ₹3.55 lakh) for age 45 and above. For financial services, it is S$3,800 (about ₹2.81 lakh) to S$5,650 (about ₹4.18 lakh).

From 1 January 2027, it becomes S$3,600 (about ₹2.66 lakh) to S$5,100 (about ₹3.77 lakh). For financial services, it becomes S$4,000 (about ₹2.96 lakh) to S$5,650 (about ₹4.18 lakh).

Employers have a limit on how many S Pass holders they can hire. It is 10% of the workforce in services, and 15% in construction, manufacturing, marine shipyard and process sectors. The employer also pays a monthly fee, called a levy, of S$650 (about ₹48,100) for each S Pass holder.

If you earn at least S$6,000 (about ₹4.44 lakh) a month, you can apply for a Dependant's Pass for your spouse and children.

Watch out: your job offer depends on your employer's quota, not only on your own profile. MOM has a free Self-Assessment Tool for the S Pass. MOM says people who pass it have about a 90% chance of approval. If you declare a qualification, it must be genuine and from a recognised institution.

03

Work Permit: For Semi-Skilled Workers

The Work Permit is for semi-skilled workers in certain sectors. These are construction, manufacturing, marine shipyard, process and services. Workers must come from approved countries.

For construction, India is on the approved list, under "Non-Traditional Sources", along with Sri Lanka, Thailand, Bangladesh and Myanmar. The worker must be between 18 and 62 years old when applying, and can keep working until age 64.

The employer pays a monthly levy for each worker. For Indian construction workers (rates from 1 January 2024), it is S$500 (about ₹37,000) for higher-skilled workers and S$900 (about ₹66,600) for basic-skilled workers. A worker without the needed certificate is charged S$900 (about ₹66,600).

Watch out: the rules are different for each sector. In other sectors, employers can hire from India only for jobs on an approved list. Check that your sector and your job are allowed before you pay anyone. The Work Permit is also usually not meant for bringing family.

Which One Fits You?

You are a manager, engineer or IT professional with a good degree and a high salary offer.

→ Look at the Employment Pass. Check the salary for your age first, then count your COMPASS points.

You are a technician or skilled staff member with a medium salary offer.

→ Look at the S Pass. Check the salary for your age, and ask if your employer has space in their quota.

You work in construction, a factory or a similar trade.

→ The Work Permit through an approved employer is the usual way.

You want to bring your spouse and children.

→ Aim for an EP, or an S Pass with a salary of at least S$6,000 (about ₹4.44 lakh) a month. The Work Permit is usually not meant for this.

How to Check Your Eligibility Before You Apply

✔

Compare your offered salary with the minimum for your age and your sector. Do not just look at the headline number.

✔

For an EP, check the salary minimum first. Then count your COMPASS points.

✔

Try MOM's Self-Assessment Tool for the S Pass before you accept an offer.

✔

If you will apply on or after 1 January 2027, use the new salary figures.

✔

Ask your employer which pass they will apply for. A real job offer must come first.

✔

Never pay anyone to "arrange" or "buy" a pass when you have no confirmed employer. That is a scam.

✔

Check the latest figures on the MOM website before you decide. These numbers change from time to time.

The Common Mistake People Make

Many people pick a pass by job title. They think, "I am an engineer, so I need an EP." But the pass depends on your salary for your age, your education and your employer's quota. The same job can get an S Pass at one salary and an EP at another.

The second mistake is using old numbers. The salary minimums go up from 1 January 2027, so always check the figures that apply on the day your employer applies.

Bottom Line

The EP is for professionals who meet a higher salary and pass the COMPASS test. The S Pass is for mid-level staff whose employer has quota space. The Work Permit is for semi-skilled workers in approved sectors and jobs. None of these gives permanent residence, and none is possible without a real employer. Check your salary against your age, check the date of application, and then choose the pass that really matches you.

FAQ

What is the main difference between EP, S Pass and Work Permit? ▼

The EP is for professionals and needs a higher salary and COMPASS points. The S Pass is for mid-level staff, with a lower salary minimum and an employer quota. The Work Permit is for semi-skilled workers in approved sectors and countries.

Can I apply for an EP or S Pass on my own? ▼

No. In practice, your employer applies. You need a confirmed job offer first. Be careful with anyone who asks you for money to "get" you a pass without naming an employer.

Do I need a degree for the Employment Pass? ▼

Not always. You need 40 points in COMPASS. A degree-level qualification gives 10 or 20 points, depending on the institution. If you do not have one, you can still reach 40 points in other ways. But you must meet the minimum salary first.

Can I bring my family to Singapore? ▼

If you hold an S Pass and earn at least S$6,000 (about ₹4.44 lakh) a month, you can apply for a Dependant's Pass for your spouse and children. EP holders can also bring family, but MOM has salary rules, so check the current rule. The Work Permit is usually not meant for family.

Are the salary minimums changing? ▼

Yes. For new applications from 1 January 2027, the EP minimum goes up to S$6,000 (about ₹4.44 lakh) to S$11,500 (about ₹8.51 lakh). For financial services, it is S$6,600 (about ₹4.88 lakh) to S$12,700 (about ₹9.40 lakh). The S Pass minimum goes up to S$3,600 (about ₹2.66 lakh) to S$5,100 (about ₹3.77 lakh). For financial services, it is S$4,000 (about ₹2.96 lakh) to S$5,650 (about ₹4.18 lakh). Renewals follow the new levels from 1 January 2028.

Can Indians get a Singapore Work Permit? ▼

Yes, for approved sectors and jobs. For example, India is on the approved list for construction. Other sectors have their own lists of approved jobs, so check that your job is on it.

Do these passes give permanent residence? ▼

No. These are temporary work passes. Permanent residence is a separate application with its own rules.
